## What was already right, and stayed right

The failures below were all in the chart-composition layer. Nothing in the design
layer was ever wrong, which the audit confirms mechanically across all nine
rendered plates:

- Every token lands. `c-1` main samples as `rgb(47, 135, 200)` = `#2F87C8` off the
  rendered SVG; ink ramp, gridline `#D8D4CC` and the accent figure label all match.
- Axis geometry is exact — 1px `ink-2` lines on the plot frame, 4px ticks pointing
  outward, tick labels 6px out, gridlines on one axis only.
- Nothing renders below 12px; no serif leaks inside a plot; no non-token color
  appears on any mark or label.
- Scatter circles carry the matched 0.8 fill/stroke opacity; bars, areas and tiles
  are opaque; lines are 2px rising to 2.4px on focus.

## Fixed

### 1. Tone-stacked charts did not stack — Figures 3B and 3C

`stackedMarks()` built **one mark per tone step**. TanStack stacks *within* a
mark, through the `z`/`color` channel; it has no concept of stacking across
marks. Every band was drawn from the baseline on top of the last — 3B's y axis
topped out at 80 and 3C's at 60, both silently, both showing overlay where the
figure claimed composition.

Now one mark carries the whole series and the tone ramp arrives as a chart-level
`color` scale (`stackedColor()` in `charts.ts`). Both plates reach 100.

### 2. The two-tone ramp ran the wrong way — Figure 3B

`TONE_STEPS.two` was `['light', 'main']`; `docs/data-vis-spec-core.md` §8b says
"one hue at **main + light**", and the plate puts Goods in the full hue at the
bottom. Only the three-tone ramp runs lightest-first, and §8c says why: it
encodes an ordered variable, so the ramp has to be monotonic. Two categories
sharing a parent are not ordered. Both ramps are now documented at the constant.

### 3. `order` was silently ignored on the palette path — Figure 3

It only ever affected the tones branch. Figure 3 looked correct because its rows
happened to arrive in the requested order. `inStackOrder()` now applies it to
both paths.

### 4. Stacked bars sat on a linear axis — Figures 3 and 3B

`yearAxis: true` selected `glAxisYear` (linear), so `barY` centred each bar on a
point position. Measured: plot x ∈ [74, 416], first bar starting at 54.45 —
19.5px left of the y-axis line — and the last running ~20px past the right edge.
Half a bar amputated at each end, and ticks nice-rounding to 2010/2015/2020
landing *between* bars.

New `glAxisYearBand`. A bar owns a slot; it is not a sample of a continuum. The
stacked-**area** path keeps its linear axis, because a band genuinely does flow
across time.

### 5. No log scale — Figures 1 and 9

`@tanstack/charts-scales` ships band, linear, ordinal and point. Both scatter
plots in the spec use a log income axis, so the gallery had been carrying a
pre-computed `log10` column and a formatter to undo it.

New `src/scales.ts`: `scaleLog`, implementing TanStack's `InferableScaleLike`, plus
a `glAxisLog` preset and `xLog` / `yLog` on `glScatterChart`. The domain rounds
outward to 1–2–5 bounds on inference — a log axis starting at 1,950 reads as an
error, and the scale contract has no separate `nice` hook to fix it later. Ticks
land on 1–2–5 steps and thin to decades only when genuinely crowded. Callers pass
raw values; the gallery's workaround column is gone.

### 6. Direct end-labels were unreachable without `highlight` — Figure 2

`glLineChart` emitted them only inside the highlight branch, so a plain
four-series line chart had to fall back to a legend — against the spec's own
"prefer these over a legend wherever the chart allows it". `endLabels: true` now
works on the multi-series path, and the wider right margin follows automatically.

Default behaviour is unchanged: on by default when highlighting (one or two
labels always have room), off by default otherwise (six could collide).

### 7. The focus label printed inside its own bubble — Figure 9

A fixed `dx: -10` clears a 6px dot and lands *inside* the mark the moment `r` is
a size channel — which is exactly the pop-up bubble chart. The offset now
resolves the actual radius per datum.

`theme.css` had defined `.gl-label--over-data` for the paper halo the spec
requires on labels over data, and nothing reached it: TanStack's `text()` mark
takes no class. New `haloLabelProps` opts a whole chart in, which is the right
granularity — a chart either places labels over marks or it doesn't.

### 8. Year axes never labelled their endpoints — Figures 2, 3, 3C, 10

D3 nicening gave 2005/2010/2015/2020 for a 2003–2024 series: neither of the years
the subtitle names. `yearTicks()` now generates values pinned to both endpoints,
preferring a step that divides the span exactly so the final gap matches every
other gap.

### 9. Ordered color scales had nowhere to live

`RectOptions.fill` and `DotOptions.fill` are plain strings in TanStack, not
channels, so a sequential or diverging encoding **cannot** be applied per datum —
it has to route through the chart-level color scale. Nothing provided one.

`scaleSequential` and `scaleDiverging` now do (`src/scales.ts`), over the
`grammar.md` ramps. The diverging scale scales each half independently so the hue
boundary sits exactly on the midpoint: a domain of [-2, +8] mapped linearly across
six steps would put the boundary at +3 and have the chart claim a midpoint it does
not have. It also warns when handed a purely positive domain, which is the most
common way a correct dataset tells a false story.

These are the first pieces of the choropleth work (Figures 7 and 8) — the
projection path is still missing.

### 10. `glChartProps` was referenced but not exported

`theme.css` documented `glChartProps({ zeroBaseline: true })` as the way to opt
into the zero-baseline promotion, and the three exported props constants were
hand-written literals of the same class strings. The builder now exists and the
constants derive from it, so a class added to one cannot silently miss the others.

## The zero baseline, twice corrected

Worth recording in full, because the answer moved twice and the second move is
still open.

The first pass flagged the promoted zero gridline as a bug: on the ECI scatter it
paints a heavy dark rule through mid-plot, and the spec's own plate has no such
rule. Then `grammar.md` §3.5 turned out to be explicit — "Zero baseline: render
with axis weight, not gridline weight" — so the flag was withdrawn: library
right, plate wrong.

It is now **opt-in** (`glChartProps({ zeroBaseline: true })`), on the reading that
the rule is about a chart whose marks grow *from* zero, not about every axis that
happens to contain zero. That reading is persuasive — and it is a departure from
the literal text of `grammar.md`, which by this repo's own convention makes the
downstream file the bug until the grammar is amended.

So it is filed as [`SPEC.md` C1](../../SPEC.md) with a
proposed amendment, rather than settled here. If the amendment is accepted it has
to propagate to the R theme and `skills/chart-audit`, not just to `theme.css`. If
it is rejected, `theme.css` reverts to promoting unconditionally.

`audit.mjs` tests the contract as implemented — the gridline at zero must be axis
weight **iff** the chart opted in — so it will hold whichever way the ruling goes,
and will catch a chart that opts in and doesn't get it.

## Still open

### Three chart types cannot be built at all

Radar (p.18), boxplot/violin (p.19–20), and both choropleths (p.21–22). `glRegion`
paints a pre-projected rect but there is no projection path and no
sequential-ramp binning.

### Questions for `grammar.md`

Filed in [`SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md) C1, A1 and C3. Two of the
three have since been answered by measuring the plates rather than asking.

1. **Is the zero-baseline rule about the value or the reference?** See above.
2. ~~**Do flat treemaps take separators?**~~ **Resolved 2026-08-06** —
   `SPEC.md` A1. Yes, at every depth. Measured off the plates themselves:
   Figures 4 and 11 both run a uniform 4.8px paper gutter on both axes, rounded
   to 5px in `SPEC.md` §3.4.1 (10px between parent blocks). Figure 11 was the
   proof — seven `c-muted` tiles with nothing but the gutter between them.
3. ~~**Paper text on `c-muted` fails WCAG AA**~~ **Resolved 2026-08-06** — but
   not the way this suggested. The plates do not use `c-muted-dark` (3.12:1,
   still short); they use near-black `ink` (8.65:1). The rule is now `paper`
   below 0.4 relative luminance and `ink` at or above it. A *different* AA gap
   survives that ruling — `paper` on `c-1` and `c-5` — and is filed as
   `SPEC.md` C3 with the numbers and three ways out.

### Deliberate divergences

Recorded so nobody "fixes" them back toward the plate:

- **Treemap opacity.** The plate draws tiles at 0.8 with dark strokes; the library
  renders full opacity, no stroke, separated by a 5px paper gutter. The PDF
  contradicts itself and `grammar.md` adjudicates — `docs/data-vis-spec-core.md` §0.
- **Treemap tile order.** The plate puts "Everything else" in the far column
  although it is the fourth-largest tile. That is hand placement; sorting
  residual buckets last hands `d3-hierarchy` a non-descending list and visibly
  degrades every tile around it, so `residual` changes tone and palette spend
  only, never order.
- **Treemap tile arrangement generally.** The plates are hand-laid and not
  squarified — the library's arrangement will not match them tile-for-tile at
  any plot size, only in aspect and gutter. Figure 4's "Everything else" also
  overruns its own right-hand padding by ~7px, which the library's fit test will
  not do; at the gallery's 596px width that tile comes out 97.5px wide against a
  89.2px estimated label, so it goes unlabelled rather than spilling.
- **Palette order.** Figure 1's third series is `c-4` purple in the plate and
  `c-3` here; Figure 2's is the same. Decision Rule 1 says in order.
- **Subtitle terminal period.** Every plate subtitle has one; Decision Rule 10
  says it should not. Still unresolved upstream.
- **Figure 2's series names.** The plate's are placeholders (`x`, `y`, `z`, `t`).
